Building an AI Resume Skill Gap Analyzer

AI · Embeddings · Semantic Similarity · Systems Thinking

Why I Built This

I wanted to go beyond simply calling AI APIs and actually understand how modern AI systems work under the hood. Terms like embeddings, semantic similarity, and vector search are used everywhere, but they often feel abstract until you build something end-to-end.

This project started with a simple question: How can a system compare a resume to a job role in a way that understands meaning, not just keywords? Building a resume skill gap analyzer became my way of answering that question through practice.

System Overview

At a high level, the system takes unstructured resume text and produces role recommendations based on semantic similarity between skills.

  1. Parse and normalize skills from a resume
  2. Represent skills as numerical vectors (embeddings)
  3. Compare resume skills to predefined role requirements
  4. Score similarity and recommend the best-matching roles

Each step is isolated into its own module, which made the system easier to test, reason about, and extend.

From Text to Numbers: Embeddings

The core idea behind this project is embeddings. An embedding is a vector (a list of numbers) that represents the meaning of a piece of text. Models like BERT learn these representations by being trained on massive amounts of language data.

Instead of treating skills as plain strings, the system converts them into vectors in a high-dimensional space. Skills with similar meanings end up closer together in that space, even if the exact words are different.

For example, terms like "machine learning", "ML", and "artificial intelligence" can be numerically close, even though they are not textually identical.

Semantic Comparison with Cosine Similarity

Once skills are represented as vectors, the question becomes: how do we compare them? This project uses cosine similarity, a common technique in machine learning.

Cosine similarity measures the angle between two vectors rather than their raw distance. This is important because we care about how similar the meanings are, not how large the vectors are.

Mathematically, cosine similarity is defined as: 

cosine_similarity(A, B) = (A · B) / (||A|| × ||B||)

A score closer to 1 means the skills are very similar, while a score closer to 0 means they are unrelated. This allows the system to make nuanced comparisons instead of binary matches.

Why Semantic Search Beats Keyword Matching

Traditional keyword matching breaks down quickly in real-world data. Resumes and job descriptions use different phrasing, abbreviations, and terminology.

Semantic similarity allows the system to understand intent and meaning rather than exact wording. This made the recommendations more realistic and aligned with how humans evaluate candidates.

However, semantic systems also introduce tradeoffs: thresholds must be tuned carefully to avoid false positives, and performance considerations become important as data scales.

Engineering Challenges

Skill Extraction and Normalization

Resumes are inconsistent by nature. Extracting meaningful skills required careful preprocessing, normalization, and validation against a known skill set. Small mistakes at this stage propagate downstream.

Choosing Similarity Thresholds

Deciding what counts as a "match" required experimentation. Too strict, and relevant skills were missed. Too loose, and unrelated skills were incorrectly matched. Iteration and testing were critical here.

Performance and Cost Awareness

Embedding generation is computationally expensive. I had to think about batching, caching, and system design to keep the application responsive while understanding the cost tradeoffs of different approaches.

Looking Forward

This project gave me confidence working with modern AI concepts at a systems level and made me feel more comfortable working with embeddings and similarity metrics.

In the future, I plan on extending the roles list to more than Data Scientist, Backend Engineer and ML Engineer and explore different types of embeddings.
